# Research for a persistent solo narrative game

The closest fit across the two bibliographies is a text game with authoritative world state, a director that selects feasible story beats, and characters that act from individual perspectives. Models can interpret free-form input, suggest actions and scenes, and write prose. The engine commits valid consequences and records who observed them. A player-facing journal can then assemble discoveries from the same events that drive the world. This is a proposed combination of results from interactive fiction, role-playing, and long-form writing research. [Orchestrated Reality](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16014) · [StoryVerse](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13042) · [Perspective-Bounded Memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25632) · [IVIE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13348)

## Coverage

Screened the indexed titles and categories in the November 2026 snapshots of **Yingpeng Ma’s Awesome Story Generation** (199 papers) and **Picrew’s Awesome LLM Story Generation** (302 entries, including projects). The counts describe each index and include overlap. The targeted review covers 30 indexed papers and two earlier drama and planning precedents. It draws on the methods and results of the central papers and uses selected sections or abstracts for adjacent leads. The selection favors interactive fiction, world and character simulation, perspective, planning, consistency, and evaluation. [Yingpeng Ma bibliography](https://github.com/yingpengma/Awesome-Story-Generation/blob/c5ecde6397b79b88b04db7d97b1b34bab5acadac/README.md) · [Picrew bibliography](https://github.com/Picrew/awesome-llm-story-generation/blob/1e9589722c761e813b3967f3926c679c7c591c5a/README.md)

In the tables, **Ma**, **Picrew**, and **Both** indicate where the item is listed. **Adjacent** marks older or complementary primary work reached independently. Paper links go to publisher pages, author-hosted copies, or the authors’ preprints. The similarly named **NCP-Bench** and **ReasoningNCP** address different questions. NCP-Bench, listed by Ma, measures *Narrative Commitment Preservation* during play. ReasoningNCP, listed by both, uses *Next-Chapter Prediction* to train chapter planning for offline writing. ## World state and constrained generation

| Work | In | Finding and implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orchestrated Reality | Picrew | Models free-form play as intent plus typed parameters over canonical JSON. The world agent plans, proposes a diff, validates and commits an atomic update, and projects a limited view to the player. Useful distinction between replaying recorded transitions and reproducing model sampling. The paper reports 15 deployment incidents and informal testing. A formal player study and concurrent NPC minds are future work. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16014) |
| World-State Transformations | Picrew | Maps flexible natural-language actions to a finite set of symbolic transformations executed by rules. Eight participants completed two small scenarios, including unusual solutions. Models also incorrectly proposed unlocks on wrong riddle answers. The practical lesson is to validate action-specific preconditions in code, alongside schema and reference checks. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24719) |
| IVIE | Both | Generates locations, items, NPCs, and puzzles incrementally with symbolic checks. Saves the initial world and turn changes for replay and diagnosis. Two of 16 generated worlds had structurally impossible objectives that passed validation because required items lacked an accessible location. Supplement connectivity checks with goal and clue reachability across likely story states. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13348) |
| PANGeA | Ma | Combines validation and retrieved memory for turn-based RPG content and NPC dialogue. In the authors’ validation study over ten scenarios, reported accuracy rose from 28% to 98% for Llama 3 8B and 71% to 99% for GPT-4. This establishes a benefit on their validation task. Campaign state integrity has separate requirements. [Published paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/31876/34043/35945) · [Authors’ earlier preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19721) |
| FactTrack and ConWriter | Both | FactTrack tracks atomic facts with time intervals and evaluates contradiction detection in outlines. ConWriter writes scene by scene, validates required transitions against a story state, and prioritizes localized repair using uncertainty signals. Its offline benchmark results motivate checking scene changes and repairing drafts before publication. [FactTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16347) · [ConWriter](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05169) |

## Director and responsive scenes

| Work | In | Finding and implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| StoryVerse | Both | The closest architecture for reconciling plot and character agency. Each abstract act has a dramatic goal, prerequisites, and bindings that carry details across acts. The director instantiates an eligible act from the current world, reviews proposed actions for executability and character motivation, and gives the floor to the character simulator when no act is eligible. The paper demonstrates two proof-of-concept domains. It also explicitly includes character thoughts in proposed actions. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13042#S2) |
| NarrativeGenie | Both | Turns a short story overview into a partially ordered beat graph, resolves event dependencies, and uses player progress to adapt dialogue. Its mystery demo provides timely hints and a final spoken synthesis of gathered evidence. Nineteen people took part in its human evaluation. The adaptive dialogue test used a fixed mystery scaffold, and some players wanted less obvious hints. [Authors’ copy](https://public.intellimedia.ncsu.edu/pubmgr/pubdb/pdfs/kumaran-aiide-2024.pdf) |
| SENNA | Ma | Uses a narrative graph to guide a solo RPG. In a study of six redirection strategies, participants preferred in-world information, consequences, and NPC influence to hard denials. This supports surfacing invitations or credible consequences when a player heads elsewhere. The study concerns a prewritten adventure. [Authors’ institution](https://vbn.aau.dk/en/publications/guiding-not-railroading-design-and-evaluation-of-a-multi-agent-sy/) |
| IBSEN, Enhanced Immersion and Agency, and Open-Theatre | Both | IBSEN delegates local portrayal to actors and has the director reschedule after player intervention. Enhanced Immersion and Agency has the director send an actor a local motivation, limits changes to one incomplete plot element per periodic reflection, and discusses the latency and depth trade-off of individual actors. Open-Theatre’s Director–Global Actor consolidates character context for efficiency and uses hierarchical retrieval. A global actor’s access to all characters’ memories makes it a poor fit for scenes that depend on strict knowledge boundaries without further controls. [IBSEN](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01093) · [Enhanced Immersion and Agency](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17878) · [Open-Theatre](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16713) |
| DOC, DOME, and PLOTTER | Both | Offline writing studies offer three useful authoring patterns. DOC controls prose with detailed outlines, DOME updates a hierarchical outline as writing proceeds, and PLOTTER checks causality in event and character graphs before writing. They suggest dependencies and limited plan revisions, with runtime player agency to be tested separately. [DOC](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.190/) · [DOME](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13575) · [PLOTTER](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21253) |
| Façade and Sabre | Adjacent | Façade organizes authored interactive drama into beats with runtime selection. Sabre’s centralized symbolic planner can pursue an authorial goal using actions that make sense for characters with their own goals and potentially false beliefs. These precedents clarify the distinction between a director’s objective and a character’s reason to act. They rely on modeled or authored domains. [Façade](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs.cmu.edu/project/oz/web/papers/CMU-CS-02-198.pdf) · [Sabre](https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/ware2021sabre.pdf) |

## Characters, private thoughts, and the journal

| Work | In | Finding and implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Staying In Character / ReverieMem | Both | Uses first-person scene memories, visibility-tagged facts, and situation-dependent personality patterns. The November v2 paper reports a 34.6 percentage-point gain in Knowledge Boundary Fidelity over its strongest reference on 4,386 questions across eight novels. Its book-based results motivate retrieval scoped by character and time, with separate tests for live gameplay and disclosure. [Paper, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25632v2) |
| Generative Agents | Picrew | Stores episodes, retrieves by relevance, recency, and importance, and uses observations, planning, and reflection. The full architecture gave the most believable behavior in the authors’ ablations. The 25-agent village also illustrates social information spreading through interaction. The authors observed retrieval failures and embellished memories. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442) |
| EvoSpark and BookWorld | Both | EvoSpark keeps immutable episode history, a mutable personality and relationship snapshot, and shared lore. BookWorld combines location-constrained role interactions with a world agent, character profiles, and retrieval. Use changing relationships and obligations alongside stable characterization. EvoSpark’s shared lore requires additional access controls for hidden facts, and BookWorld’s source-book fidelity results do not directly measure play in a new world. [EvoSpark](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12776) · [BookWorld](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14538) |
| OmniToM and ArcANE | Picrew | OmniToM scores explicit actor-specific beliefs and their access, truth, and source, exposing errors that a correct final answer can hide. ArcANE finds that arc context up to the current chapter improves stage-appropriate portrayal in all evaluated models. These support tests for mistaken beliefs, relay of a rumor, motive changes after a specific event, and future-arc leakage. [OmniToM](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26322) · [ArcANE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05553) |
| Narrative World Model and Storyline Trees | Ma / Picrew, respectively | Narrative World Model’s temporal graph distinguishes event from reveal, tracks who knows a secret, and links setups to payoffs. Storyline Trees supports scene-level evidence retrieval and higher-level summaries on benchmark QA. These are useful models for a campaign journal whose entries link to discoverable source events and whose scene summaries can be rebuilt. The papers evaluate retrieval and QA. Journal usability and spoiler safety still need direct testing. [Narrative World Model](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05577) · [Storyline Trees](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20900) |
| NstAgent and CHIRON | Picrew / Both, respectively | NstAgent tracks character states, past events, and outstanding narrative promises with typed updates in offline long-form generation. CHIRON validates rich character sheets extracted from fiction. The first suggests maintaining open threads explicitly in director memory, and the second suggests keeping stable profile fields grounded in evidence. Neither evaluation tests campaign turn resolution. [NstAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35759) · [CHIRON](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.499/) |

## Implications for a playable prototype

1. **Resolve actions against a determinate world.** Give locations, people, objects, and clues stable identifiers. Parse the player’s intent into a proposal, resolve ambiguity when it changes the outcome, and check invariants and action preconditions before applying effects. Build the narration from validated outcomes and permissible observations. Scope model repair to the rejected proposal or draft prose. A saved game should replay recorded decisions and consequences in order. [Orchestrated Reality](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16014) · [World-State Transformations](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24719) · [IVIE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13348)

2. **Give each actor its own perspective.** Track observations and beliefs with timestamps and provenance, including who said what to whom. Record rumor and uncertainty as properties of belief. Store simulated inner thoughts as short private character data, with explicit channels for speech, visible behavior, and optional public narration. Gate retrieval by visibility before ranking and establish explicit delivery events for disclosures. For example, retrieving a letter changes its location, reading it changes the reader’s knowledge, and reporting it can update a listener’s belief. A character outside the room retains their prior belief until an in-world event changes it. [Perspective-Bounded Memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25632v2) · [OmniToM](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26322) · [StoryVerse](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13042#S2)

3. **Direct opportunities and preserve consequences.** Represent arcs as prerequisites, possible scenes, open threads, and outcomes that can be fulfilled or lost. Keep fixed world commitments and flexible plot goals explicit. Let the director select eligible scenes and respond to surprising decisions with consequences, later opportunities, or a different outcome. A small scenario can exercise a few recurring NPCs, one hidden fact, one evolving relationship, and several compatible routes to a turning point. [StoryVerse](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13042#S2) · [NarrativeGenie](https://public.intellimedia.ncsu.edu/pubmgr/pubdb/pdfs/kumaran-aiide-2024.pdf) · [SENNA](https://vbn.aau.dk/en/publications/guiding-not-railroading-design-and-evaluation-of-a-multi-agent-sy/)

4. **Build the journal from what the player has seen or written.** Display discovered clues, open leads, known people, and concise scene entries linked to player-visible event IDs. Preserve authored notes and distinguish reports or suspicions from verified discoveries. Exclude unrevealed world events and private thoughts at the input to the journal projection. Keep a separate diagnostic event history for replay and debugging. The journal design is an inference from traceability, narrative retrieval, and adaptive evidence recaps. The cited studies leave the journal’s usefulness in a persistent solo game open. [IVIE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13348) · [Narrative World Model](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05577) · [NarrativeGenie](https://public.intellimedia.ncsu.edu/pubmgr/pubdb/pdfs/kumaran-aiide-2024.pdf)

## Evaluation

- **World and commitment integrity.** Use a recorded run to check inventory, location, chronology, reveal conditions, and stage prerequisites after every turn. Include ordinary play and inputs that challenge past facts or depart from a planned scene. NCP-Bench, from Ma’s list, derives 100 environments from movie synopses and uses model auditors to track facts, commitments, and whether the narrator addresses player intent. Its best tested narrator achieved 42% survival at turn 20 under adversarial interventions. The number is specific to that benchmark. As its memory ablation illustrates, a system can improve commitment tracking while losing fidelity to the current player action. [NCP-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160)
- **Knowledge and character.** Use a secret-transfer scenario with a witness, an absent character, a rumor, and a later correction. Inspect the actual belief state and public output after each event. Assess whether a recurring character’s behavior follows their motivation and relationship history, alongside dialogue quality. CharacterBox, from Ma, evaluates behavior trajectories in a text world. The Picrew-listed OmniToM and ArcANE provide complementary targets for false belief and time-appropriate characterization. [CharacterBox](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05631) · [OmniToM](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26322) · [ArcANE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05553)
- **Play quality.** Track whether the game keeps moving, respects canon, develops meaningfully, and gives the player consequential choices. WSE-bench, from Ma, measures generation coverage, consistency, and richness separately. NARRA-Gym, listed by both, uses interactive persona-driven sessions and finds that human and model judges can rank middling systems differently. Test the journal and the sense of agency with players as well as automated checks. [WSE-bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15654) · [NARRA-Gym](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08503)

The largest evidence gap is the combined system. The papers independently evaluate pieces of world consistency, character portrayal, agency, and memory. The proposed journal, strict disclosure rules, latency, and authoring effort need to be assessed together in a playable scenario.

## Public repositories and reuse

The strongest code reuse candidate is the **published VerseCraft prototype**. Inspect its turn finalizer, knowledge filters, and tests as isolated components. **Storylet Studio** is the strongest small dependency candidate if maintaining beat prerequisites and effects becomes an authoring bottleneck. **Project Lunar** and **Kataki** are particularly useful comparisons for how the experience should feel and what the journal and author tools should expose. Keep Harbinger's world rules, belief semantics, and journal projection in a small first-party core. Try the VerseCraft code against the absent-witness scene in the [system proposal](architecture.md). The reuse test is whether its candidate components can preserve evidence provenance, prevent premature disclosures, and replay accepted outcomes without its setting-specific logic or hosted infrastructure. If those components couple tightly to the larger project, use their fixtures as examples and implement the narrow contract. That preserves a path to modular reuse while keeping the first episode tractable.
