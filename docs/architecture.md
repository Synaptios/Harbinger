# Harbinger narrative system proposal

Harbinger is a solo interactive drama for free-form play in an established fictional world. The player controls what their protagonist says, thinks, and attempts. Characters and world rules determine the outcomes. The first milestone is one complete episode worth replaying because a relationship could unfold differently. Build it as a local web app with portable saves, then use completed playthroughs to decide whether to invest in a hosted product.

AI Dungeon is the reference for entering actions freely and reading the resulting story. Its documented combination of recent turns, durable story guidance, Story Cards, and retrieved memories is useful context for how to present a long-running narrative. Harbinger will assemble each model call from an explicit world state and an individual viewpoint. The [research review](research.md) covers the broader bibliography and public implementations. [AI Dungeon context guide](https://help.aidungeon.com/faq/what-goes-into-the-context-sent-to-the-ai)

## Established setting and first episode

Use **Arthurian Britain as told in Thomas Malory’s *Le Morte Darthur***. Pin the Everyman’s Library text and its Book XXI chapter numbering for the prototype. Start in London as Guenever reaches the Tower, before Mordred learns she has taken refuge there. The succession crisis supplies published places, institutions, relationships, and disputed news against which to test the system. Malory records that Mordred had letters made claiming Arthur died fighting Launcelot, that he was crowned at Canterbury, and that Guenever used wedding preparations as a pretext to travel to London. Arthur’s later arrival at Dover belongs to the setting’s future at the start of play. [Caxton edition record](https://www.themorgan.org/incunables/134886) · [Everyman’s edition imprint](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_Morte_d%27Arthur/Volume_II) · [Book XXI, chapter I](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_Morte_d%27Arthur/Volume_II/Book_XXI/Chapter_I) · [Book XXI, chapter II](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_Morte_d%27Arthur/Volume_II/Book_XXI/Chapter_II)

**The Bread Ledger** is the proposed original episode within that setting. The player is a London household clerk with access to a merchant’s delivery books. Alice, a baker who once stood surety for the clerk, has been sending flour to the Tower. A guild officer needs the ledger before dusk to keep the other bakers’ stalls open under Mordred’s officials. The entries could expose Alice and the people helping her. Give the officer a reason to believe the city is safer under Mordred. Malory records that many people thought of Arthur’s reign as a time of war and welcomed Mordred. Play determines who is exposed, who gets supplies through, and whether Alice trusts the clerk afterward. [Malory’s account of public sentiment](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_Morte_d%27Arthur/Volume_II/Book_XXI/Chapter_I)

Begin with London and the Tower as established places, a small authored network of streets and interiors, a compact recurring cast, and one approved slot each for a generated person and place. Keep the medieval source, authored local additions, and session events identifiable in the world pack. Settle names and spelling variants during authoring. Mark future canon with an effective time and give initial knowledge only to characters with a plausible route to it. Store exact source passages or references in the test fixtures, with independently written questions that include plausible mistakes from other versions of the legend.

| Lore test | Expected behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Public news with a false premise | A Londoner who has heard the proclamation may believe Arthur died. The engine knows the letters were commissioned by Mordred and preserves the proclamation as a claim. A clerk who merely saw the notice can name its claim without knowing who arranged it. |
| Two viewpoints at the same time | A household insider briefed on the plan can describe why supplies are needed. A guild officer who only heard the shopping explanation reasons from that report. Holding the scene constant and changing who was briefed changes the permissible testimony. |
| Cross-version and future details | Geoffrey of Monmouth gives Arthur a report that Guanhumara married Modred. In Malory’s setting, a similar assertion can enter play as a claim, while Guenever’s refusal remains canon. Arthur’s return at Dover requires a later observation or report for a character to know it. [Geoffrey’s account](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Six_Old_English_Chronicles.djvu/292) · [Malory’s account](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_Morte_d%27Arthur/Volume_II/Book_XXI/Chapter_I) |
| Played memory alongside lore | Alice’s belief about the clerk changes after an observed disclosure or a credible report. Reopen the save several scenes later and test both her response to the broken promise and her recall of an unrelated established fact. |

Score exact recall, knowledge boundaries, source attribution, and chronological validity independently. Use public, obscure, disputed, and future facts with the same model in a transcript-and-summary baseline and in Harbinger. Use paired tests where only witness access changes, so correct answers from model familiarity alone cannot pass the knowledge-boundary check. **ReverieMem** was selected because it tests who can know a fact within a published novel and uses visibility-scoped memories. **FANToM** and **OmniToM** supply complementary cases for unequal access, false belief, and belief provenance. Their evaluations motivate Harbinger’s fixtures. This episode still needs testing as interactive fiction. [ReverieMem](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25632v2) · [FANToM](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.890/) · [OmniToM](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26322)

## World state, character knowledge, and thoughts

A versioned world pack defines the starting canon, map and travel costs, schedules, authored NPCs, eligible additions, and dramatic situations. SQLite stores the active save and an ordered record of accepted events. Separate records keep character knowledge and the reader’s view grounded in those events.

| Record | What it owns |
| --- | --- |
| Canon and world state | Established facts, chronology, current positions and custody, and the starting conditions for each session. Track when mutable facts were true. |
| Observation and disclosure | Who saw, heard, read, received, or was told something, when, and through which event. Presence, audibility, sight lines, and delivery rules determine recipients. |
| Belief and memory | What a character accepts, doubts, or suspects, with confidence, source, acquisition time, and references to observations. Hearing a claim and believing its contents are distinct changes. Conflicting reports can coexist. |
| Character state | Stable voice examples, obligations, goals, limits, directed relationships, emotional state, and routines. Changes in mood or trust should have perceptible causes. |
| Narrative agenda | Scheduled developments, eligible situations, upcoming consequences, and open promises or questions. A thread can close through action, inaction, or changed circumstances. |
| Private thought | A short first-person fictional passage for a specific NPC, recorded against the scene or offstage event at which it formed. Keep it with structured intent or appraisal in private state. |

Build each NPC context from the character’s allowed facts, memories, and recent observations. Filter before ranking or summarizing. Feed the narrator and journal separate projections built for the protagonist. A model may suggest an inference, lie, or changed belief. The engine checks access, preserves the claim’s source, and delivers it only to its actual audience. Use stable fact and entity IDs to make access and changes traceable. Start with indexed relational queries by character, topic, and time. Add semantic search if playtests show grounded callbacks are being missed. [FactTrack on temporal facts](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16347) · [ReverieMem on viewpoint-scoped recall](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25632v2)

Write an interior passage for **each materially relevant NPC**, including an offstage actor whose decision or receipt of news affects the turn. A passerby carrying out a routine can stay in the schedule. Use a brief thought keyed to that person’s current perspective, such as *I can let them take the flour. I cannot let them read the names.* Alice can feel fear or misjudge the clerk. Her thought may inform her conduct, and only a permissible expression, visible action, or later disclosure reaches another character. Create each thought once with the turn, using the same bounded actor call as the intention where possible. An authored first-person fallback covers an unavailable actor call. **Generative Agents** was selected for the role of observation, planning, and reflection in its believability evaluation. It also reports recall errors and invented embellishments, which is why an interior reflection is stored as one person’s perspective. **StoryVerse** supplies a direct precedent for fictional thoughts alongside character actions. [Generative Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442) · [StoryVerse method](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13042#S2)

## Active narrative director

Author a dramatic question, specific pressures on the cast, possible local outcomes, and situation templates with prerequisites, roles, locations, paths of discovery, and consequences. The director manages two connected functions.

1. **Orchestrate the world.** Maintain a world-time agenda for deadlines, messages, NPC routines, and offstage decisions. Advance it as the player spends time in the fiction. Resolve due events from world conditions and each participant’s goals and knowledge, including events the player chooses to miss. Browsing the journal or taking time to compose a response leaves fictional time where it is.
2. **Bring a compelling situation to the player.** Select from eligible developments based on the player’s current pursuit, urgency, unresolved relationships, and recent pacing. The director can send a courier through an actual route, let a relationship change become apparent in the next encounter, or put someone in a position to ask for an answer. A significant offstage event creates a trace the player can discover. At the next plausible opportunity, surface one consequence through a person, object, or change the protagonist can perceive. Give it a decision or an emotional cost of its own.

If the clerk skips the ledger inspection, the officer can proceed with the records available. Later, Alice arrives with unsold bread and asks the clerk to walk her home. Her shuttered stall signals something changed. The player can ask what happened, seek the officer’s written order, help Alice with her immediate problem, or keep going. The inspection has already happened. Alice’s invitation and her willingness to explain depend on her knowledge and relationship with the clerk.

Start with explicit prerequisites and simple ranking for urgency, player relevance, relationship stakes, and repetition. Log which situation was chosen and why in the testing view. An optional model can propose staging within an eligible situation. State validators check timing, access, actor roles, and facts. NPCs choose how they respond and the player retains control of their protagonist. **StoryVerse** and **Façade** were selected for the division between dramatic planning and the behavior that realizes a scene. **SENNA** was selected because its solo RPG study compared redirection approaches and found value in in-world information, consequences, and NPC influence. **NarrativeGenie** shows how beat dependencies and progress-aware hints can support a mystery, with some players preferring subtler guidance. These precedents support a director that can move events and vary how visibly it intervenes. [StoryVerse](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13042#S2) · [Façade](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~michaelm/publications/mateas-aiide2005.pdf) · [SENNA](https://vbn.aau.dk/en/publications/guiding-not-railroading-design-and-evaluation-of-a-multi-agent-sy/) · [NarrativeGenie](https://public.intellimedia.ncsu.edu/pubmgr/pubdb/pdfs/kumaran-aiide-2024.pdf)

## Turn and save processing

1. **Interpret.** Parse free text into ordered attempted actions and dialogue using the protagonist’s viewpoint. Preserve the player’s dialogue where possible. Ask for clarification when ambiguity changes a consequential outcome. The interpreter can suggest what the player meant. World rules determine what is possible.
2. **Resolve until the next decision.** Check each step against world rules, advance time by its actual duration, resolve due events in stable order, and ask relevant actors for scoped intentions and first-person thoughts. At the first consequential interruption the protagonist can perceive, finish the immediate event and stop before executing later steps or advancing past that moment. An attempt to take the ledger and leave can end with Alice catching the player reaching for it. The player composes a fresh response.
3. **Render from validated outcomes.** Provide the narrator with the player-visible scene packet, including permissible speech and references to any reveals. Keep the protagonist’s voluntary actions and inner life under player control. Use approved phrasing for consequential clues and a factual fallback if generated prose adds unsupported details or fails validation. Derive the journal changes from the same authorized events.
4. **Commit once.** Save the events, thoughts, accepted prose, and journal projection together against the expected save revision. Keep provider calls outside the transaction. Retry with the same request ID after a network interruption and return the already committed turn if one exists. On reconnect, check the revision before displaying a failure or sending a second action. Retain the draft and last confirmed passage when a turn fails before commit.

**Orchestrated Reality** and **World-State Transformations** were selected for their use of structured intents and validated changes over authoritative state. The latter’s reported incorrect unlocks motivate action-specific checks as well as schema validation. Keep the event record for diagnostics, load verification, and testing. The player resumes from the most recent committed turn. [Orchestrated Reality](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16014) · [World-State Transformations](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24719) · [SQLite transactions](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_transaction.html)

**Duplicate save** makes an independent copy of the current committed state under a new save ID. Include narrative history, the world-pack version, the clock and pending events, actor knowledge and thoughts, and the journal. If a turn is pending, enable duplication when its result is settled. Keep the original open and offer to open the named copy. Any subsequent edits or turns affect the selected save alone. History is read-only. The only starting state for a copy is the latest committed revision of its source save.

A portable save package includes private state and is labeled accordingly at export. Provide a separate shareable story export built from player-visible content. Validate the package structure and world-pack version before import, and import as a new save. Test reopening a complete package in a clean local installation.

## Constrained NPC and location generation

The author defines available slots with a parent location or institution, a role or place archetype, capacity, connections, naming rules, initial knowledge, and allowed effects. Choose a compatible slot and stable identity first. Generate local detail inside those limits, validate references and travel, and commit the instance once. Repeated visits use the same person, location, and consequences. For example, a guild porter can staff a permitted riverside storehouse and know its delivery routine. Learning about Alice’s route requires an event or an authored source. Keep discoveries, obligations, and access routes reachable across the episode’s likely outcomes. **IVIE** was selected for its incremental symbolic world generation and its reported structurally impossible objectives despite map connectivity. This motivates tests for reaching clues and outcomes through the actions a player can actually take. [IVIE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13348)

## Reading, journal, and testing interface

Design the site as a reading app. Give the passage a comfortable line length, generous spacing, high-contrast type, and a clear heading with the current place and fictional time. Keep the labeled multiline composer in easy reach. Use a visible **Continue** button and preserve Enter for newlines. Suggestions, when shown, should express actions the protagonist could reasonably infer from their surroundings.

| Surface | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Story | One reading column with the composer and the latest turn status. Follow new text when the reader is already near the end. Otherwise preserve their position and offer a **New passage** link. |
| Journal | On wide screens, an adjacent panel that keeps the story usable. On phones, an explicitly opened sheet that restores reading position and draft on close. Show people, places, clues, promises, and notes. Label a claim as reported or firsthand, show when it is disputed, and link each entry or correction to its source passage. Mark new discoveries and link to the changes. |
| Testing | A separate opt-in workspace from save settings, with a clear spoiler label and a return path to the same reading position. List relevant NPCs by scene or turn and display each first-person thought under the NPC’s name. Allow inspection of observation sources and director choices for diagnosing behavior. Opening, sorting, and closing the view do not advance time or change the save. |
| Save library | Open, name, duplicate, import, and export local saves. Show which save is active and when it was last committed. Keep the action to open a duplicate explicit. |

Build the testing workspace from a separate private read path. Ordinary play responses, page data, journal search, suggestions, counts, and shareable exports receive only the player-facing projection. The testing view can show the reader what Alice thinks. Harbinger still treats the protagonist as unaware until something happens in the fiction. A player can attempt to act on a guess, and the world responds to the attempt without confirming hidden information by default. **Narrative World Model** was selected for its distinction between an event and its later reveal. That distinction makes source-linked journal entries, rumor tracking, and a separate testing surface possible. Journal usability is a playtest question. [Narrative World Model](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05577)

Make these web UX requirements part of the prototype:

- **Accessible reading and controls.** Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Check keyboard-only play, visible focus, focus clear of the pinned composer, 4.5:1 contrast for ordinary text, touch targets of at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels with more room where practical, and reading and composing at 320 CSS pixels without sideways scrolling. Test text-spacing overrides and avoid communicating status through color alone. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- **Predictable overlays.** Keep the desktop journal nonmodal. If the mobile sheet is modal, give it an accessible name, move focus inside it, contain Tab, support Escape and a visible close button, and restore focus to the opener or the selected source passage. Preserve draft and reading position across layout changes. [W3C modal dialog pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/)
- **Clear signals during a long turn.** Acknowledge submission immediately. Keep the previous passage and journal available while generating, announce meaningful status changes with a polite live status, and show a retry on failure with the draft intact. Keep progress indeterminate when duration is unknown. Publish the new passage and journal together and avoid reading the entire update aloud automatically. [W3C status messages guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages) · [Nielsen on response times](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)
- **Measure interface speed and generation separately.** Use the Core Web Vitals good threshold for Interaction to Next Paint, 200 ms or less at the 75th percentile, once field measurement makes sense. In the local prototype, profile the composer, journal, and save switching independently of the provider. Track median and 95th-percentile generation time, retries, and cost per completed episode. [Google’s Web Vitals guidance](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)

## Model roles in Covel

The Bread Ledger uses Character for bounded dialogue and private thought selection and State for interpreting actions and proposing world details. The director chooses among eligible consequences using the current world state, so a missed inspection can still reach the player through a witness or a posted order. One turn can resolve several actions in order. Independent private thoughts run concurrently and keep their first-person defaults if the Character provider stalls. [Voyage model catalog](https://alpha.voyage.io/models) · [Covel role settings](https://github.com/ackness/covel/blob/013ee2ccb7f58fdfb3f05157cf408ccb3e90d891/docs/en/reference/slots.md) · [StoryVerse](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13042)

The long-range pacing director reviews the story after a randomly drawn interval of 10–20 resolved player turns. The [universal scout](../covel/pacing-director.mjs) reads earlier milestones, recent actions, sourced public evidence, and the progress of existing pursuits. It proposes several ways NPCs or the world could develop an eligible avenue. A second call applies the [game prompt](../covel/worlds/bread-ledger/pacing-prompt.mjs) and chooses which proposal to pursue. Both calls share the dedicated pacing role. The world continues its current pursuit between reviews.

The [Bread Ledger avenues](../covel/worlds/bread-ledger/story-avenues.mjs) give NPCs staged actions around bread supply, guild testimony, and flour accountability. Their earliest beats are two, eight, and eighteen resolved turns after activation. A later selection can wait while an active pursuit finishes. NPC developments the player misses can surface as sourced reports on a later encounter. The narrator gives these developments emphasis while the engine keeps the player's actions, available choices, and authored endings authoritative. The saved clock and active pursuit travel with complete exports. A short route can reach its ending before the first review is due.

The save records who observed each event, when a fact was disclosed, and which reports each character heard. An episode can give Alice an account of Guenever’s refuge while Martin continues to work from his wedding-supplies ledger. When the clerk tells him what Alice said, he gains the clerk’s account and its source. An explicit player action is required for that disclosure. A private quotation reaches only its addressed character. [Perspective-Bounded Memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25632v2)

Hindsight indexes committed evidence in one bank per save and character. Its recall results rank document references. Harbinger resolves each reference against the character’s current records, then gives Character the attributed, shareable evidence and the words addressed to that character. For supported questions about specific records, Character selects from voiced replies tied to a record the speaker can access. Character prefers unused wording for the same eligible record and uses the speaker’s recent dialogue to answer new questions in context. Harbinger checks response structure and applies only meeting selections offered by the world. Generated speech enters memory as attributed testimony. A retrieval failure leaves the actor’s local records available. [Hindsight memory banks](https://hindsight.vectorize.io/developer/api/memory-banks) · [Hindsight recall API](https://hindsight.vectorize.io/developer/api/recall)

The Bread Ledger narrator publishes resolved events and dialogue in order, then uses scene-specific material to aim for 200–300 words. It prefers scene details that have never appeared in the current save, then uses the least-used eligible detail when fresh material runs short. Older and restored saves rebuild this history from their rendered passages. The live game waits for provider responses. The benchmark records waits beyond the former stage cutoffs and includes them in time to commit. NovelOS reviews exported test transcripts after play. Covel Studio worlds use the standard Covel narrator, Qwen3 14B for rating, and Mistral NeMo for older public passages. GPT‑5.6 Luna supports Studio world creation and revision. Committed passages and their visible cast drive background images and speech. [NovelOS](https://github.com/mrigankad/NovelOS/tree/44106062149b5f179da683cb65e4574e75411ffe) · [Voyage Planning](https://alpha.voyage.io/models/inworld-gpt-5-6-luna-voyage) · [Voyage Memory](https://alpha.voyage.io/models/aid-story-summary-12b) · [Voyage Safety](https://alpha.voyage.io/models/vast-qwen-3-5-4b-safety-050726-50280287)

## Build and verification

Build one fixed-response route through the episode first, including a missed inspection, its aftermath, and a meaningful ending. Add the provider adapter and NPC thoughts, then implement journal, testing, and save controls against their separate data views. Test one generated location and character before increasing the cast. Use the published MIT VerseCraft prototype’s turn finalizer and fact-filter fixtures as the first selective reuse candidate. Adopt a component if it supports the source-backed knowledge cases and turn boundary without pulling in its setting or deployment stack. [Published VerseCraft source](https://github.com/bei666qi-pan/404-world) · [Repository comparison](research.md#public-repositories-and-reuse)

| Gate | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Canon and viewpoints | Run the source-backed fixtures, deliberate false assertions, delayed reports, and paired witness variants. Check world truth, actor prompts, actor behavior, and player projections independently. Require zero canonical state violations and unauthorized disclosures on the fixture set. |
| Thoughts | A relevant onstage NPC and an offstage NPC both get first-person entries. Seed a unique private detail. It is visible through the testing workspace, stays out of player-facing surfaces, and enters play only through a valid observation or disclosure. Opening the workspace leaves the saved revision unchanged. |
| Director and turn boundary | Play direct, evasive, and passive routes. Skipping the inspection changes the world and produces a fresh encounter or lead. The first consequential interruption preserves elapsed time and immediate effects and leaves the remainder of the requested action unexecuted. |
| Persistence and responsiveness | Compare duplicate saves after different next turns, export and restore on a clean install, and retry a failed or disconnected turn without duplicate effects. Complete one episode on a wide screen and a narrow screen with keyboard and screen reader checks. |
| Narrative quality | Compare the same episode and model against a transcript-and-summary baseline. Ask readers why Alice changed her behavior, which choice mattered, and whether the journal helped them find a clue or remember a promise. Record unsupported reasonable actions and whether readers want another playthrough. |

**NCP-Bench** was selected for tests that separate commitment preservation from fidelity to what the player asked to do. **NARRA-Gym** adds an interactive evaluation precedent and reports that human and model rankings can differ. Use automated checks for state and disclosure, then use readers to judge whether the story is moving and whether the director made it more interesting. Before a hosted release, check session economics, data isolation and export, and distribution rights for the selected edition, assets, and dependencies. [NCP-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08160) · [NARRA-Gym](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08503)
